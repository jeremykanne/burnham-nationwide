From make-up companies like Glossier to rapper/fashion designer Kanye West, or local favorites; pop-up shops promote by posting teasers on social media platforms and fulfilling through unique experiences; leaving customers wanting more.

Whether it’s a large company, a local favorite restaurant or an online store looking for temporary space, if there is a demand, it’ll happen. The question remains, how does one make it happen?

**The Process**  
In the City of Chicago, the first step is determining what the business will sell, i.e. retail, food, or a combination of both. If the business will just include retail, a license application can be submitted for either a *Retail or Tier I Pop-Up License* or a <em>Tier II or Tier III Pop-Up License.</em> The licenses are not connected to any one location, leaving endless possibilities. If a pop-up plans to be at multiple sites at once, the user will need to apply for a license per location.

The moment food is brought into the equation, whether it’s pre-packaged or not, a *Pop-Up Food Establishment User – Tier I License* will need to be applied for. There are license exceptions that are detailed on the first page [here](https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/bacp/Small%20Business%20Center/sbcfactsheets/Pop-Up_Host_Fact_Sheet_01-22-19.pdf) that may allow you to even skip this process. Not only does the license change, but now zoning gets involved. A pop-up involving food **cannot** operate in a residential or manufacturing district. If a user is unsure of the zoning requirements, check out the [City of Chicago Zoning Map](https://gisapps.cityofchicago.org/ZoningMapWeb/?liab=1&config=zoning) and avoid zoning districts “M” and “R”.

Following these basic steps will help make the process run smoothly. If you’re unsure what route to go through or have questions regarding licensing, permitting or zoning reach out to our team!

Is the World's Largest Starbucks in Chicago still open today? Yes - the Starbucks Reserve Roastery on Michigan Avenue, which opened November 15, 2019, remains a major operating destination and tourist draw on the Magnificent Mile, still holding its title as the largest Starbucks location in the world. How many floors does the Starbucks Reserve Roastery actually have? It's five stories spanning roughly 35,000 square feet - slightly different from the four-story, 43,000-square-foot figure in this post (43,000 sq ft appears to reflect the total project/building scope rather than just the finished Roastery space itself). What made this project particularly complex from a permitting standpoint? It involved converting a former retail flagship (Crate & Barrel) into a multi-story food and beverage destination with on-site roasting equipment, multiple bars, a rooftop terrace, and a custom curved escalator - the first of its kind in the Midwest - all within a historic, high-traffic stretch of Michigan Avenue requiring both standard building permits and special public way permits. Why would a project like this need special public way permits in addition to a standard building permit? Construction work on a site directly along a major, heavily trafficked corridor like Michigan Avenue often requires coordination beyond the building itself - covering things like sidewalk or lane impacts during construction, signage, and any work affecting the public right-of-way immediately surrounding the building. What can other large-scale, high-visibility renovation projects learn from how this one was permitted? Acting as a single point of contact across multiple phases and city departments, maintaining regular status updates with the project team, and being present in city plan review meetings to address issues as they arose were key elements in keeping a complex, multi-phase renovation on schedule.
