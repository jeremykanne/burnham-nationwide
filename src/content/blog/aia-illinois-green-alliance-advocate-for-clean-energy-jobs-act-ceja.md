The American Institute of Architects (AIA) has partnered with [Illinois Green Alliance](https://illinoisgreenalliance.org/)to advocate and establish sustainability through the [Clean Energy Jobs Act (CEJA)](https://ilcleanjobs.org/who-we-are/clean-energy-jobs-act/). They're taking decisive action on the climate crisis by putting Illinois on a path to 100% renewable energy. If passed, the act will allow efficiency programs to fix health and safety problems. At the moment, Massachusetts is the only state to have [passed a progressive climate change bill](https://www.mass.gov/news/governor-baker-signs-climate-legislation-to-reduce-greenhouse-gas-emissions-protect-environmental-justice-communities) and Illinois works to catch up. 

## Clean Energy Jobs Act (CEJA) advocacy and goals 

-   Energy Efficiency

-   Dramatic expansion of gas efficiency programs 

-   Approximately gain $700 million per year in net benefits 

-   Focused on building shell measures 

-   Electric Energy Efficiency 

-   Extends utility savings targets indefinitely

-   Solar

-   Essentially restarts Illinois’ solar industry 

-   Triple the size of Illinois Solar for All
-   Rooftop solar will significantly pencil out under CEJA 

-   Equity 

-   Will ensure that all workers, communities and businesses benefit equitably from every program and policy in the bill 

-   Stretch Energy Code; Building Performance Standards

-   Model Codes used to establish energy efficiency requirements: 

-   International Energy Conservation Code (IECC) and ASHRAE

-   Stretch codes provide road maps for future codes and provide learning opportunities for what works/what doesn’t. 
-   If CEJA passes, a Task Force will be established that will provide recommendations for Stretch Code. 

-   Includes: environmental justice, local government, energy efficiency expert, home-builders association, enforcement: code official or energy rater, environmental advocate, investor-owned utilities (IOUs), architect/engineers, commercial building industry, organized labor and other experts as Capital Development Board (CDB) deems necessary.

### Status of CEJA 

At the moment, the Bill has passed out of the IL House Energy and Environment Committee and will need to pass both House and Senate by midnight, May 31, 2021.

Did CEJA actually pass in 2021 like this post was advocating for? Yes, eventually - it missed the May 31, 2021 deadline mentioned in this post, but the Illinois General Assembly passed CEJA later that year, and Governor Pritzker signed it into law on September 15, 2021 as Public Act 102-0662. Did the Stretch Energy Code Task Force this post mentions actually happen? Yes - and it's produced real results. Illinois developed a 2023 Stretch Energy Code that municipalities can voluntarily adopt (Evanston became the first to do so, in 2025), and a further-improved 2026 Stretch Energy Code is in its final development stages, expected to be adoptable by Q3 2026. Is Illinois still working toward 100% clean energy, or did momentum stall after CEJA? It's continued building, not stalled. In January 2026, Illinois passed a follow-up law - the Clean and Reliable Grid Affordability Act (CRGA) - which expands on CEJA with new energy storage and grid resource-planning provisions, partly in response to rising electricity demand from data centers. Did CEJA's solar expansion goals (like tripling Illinois Solar for All) materialize? Illinois' solar industry has grown substantially since CEJA passed, with continued state-administered incentive rounds; specific program metrics (like exact Solar for All growth) are worth checking against current Illinois EPA or IPA reporting if you need precise current figures for this update. Was Massachusetts really the only state with major climate legislation at the time, and has that changed? That was accurate as of this post's 2021 framing, but the landscape has changed dramatically since - most U.S. states now have some form of climate or clean energy legislation on the books, making the original "Illinois playing catch-up to Massachusetts" framing outdated.
