A Temporary Driveway Permit is a requirement that allows construction vehicles egress to the specified construction site from the public way via a fence opening during construction to access the job site. Both a temporary driveway and corresponding construction permit are required to access a job site throughout all phases of construction.

Can an Existing Driveway and Driveway Permit be used to Access Construction Sites?

Unfortunately, they cannot. Existing poured driveways are not designed for heavy construction vehicles, and as such, these vehicles are prohibited from construction site egress through a regular driveway. Accessing a construction site without maintaining a temporary driveway permit may result in work stoppages, project delays and fines.

What is the Cost of a Temporary Driveway Permit?

Typically cost depend on the size of the driveway - mainly the width - and the length of time it is needed.

For example in Chicago, each 25 ft. section of a temporary driveway, the city charges $130.00 per month; and a 75 ft. temporary driveway permit will cost $390.00 per month.

These permits may also be subject to lost meter fee revenue by the city. This will occur if any metered parking spaces are impacted/blocked by the temporary driveway. The professionals at Burnham can help determine if this applies and what the associated costs might be.

Can I use my building's existing driveway for construction vehicle access? No - existing poured driveways aren't built to handle the weight of construction vehicles, so they're off-limits for that purpose even if they already have a valid permit. A separate Temporary Driveway Permit and fence opening are required specifically for construction site access. How much does a Temporary Driveway Permit cost in Chicago? Cost scales with width and duration - as a reference point, a 25-foot section has historically run around $130/month, with wider permits (e.g., 75 feet) costing proportionally more (~$390/month). Since CDOT updates its fee schedule annually, confirm the current rate on the city's published schedule before budgeting. What happens if I access a construction site without a Temporary Driveway Permit? You risk work stoppages, project delays, and fines - inspectors can shut down site access entirely if they find construction vehicles using an unpermitted entry point. Are there hidden costs beyond the base Temporary Driveway Permit fee? Possibly - if your temporary driveway blocks metered parking spaces, the city can charge for the lost meter revenue on top of the standard permit fee. It's worth checking this before finalizing your site logistics plan. Does a Temporary Driveway Permit cover the entire construction period automatically? No - it's billed monthly based on how long it's needed, so the total cost is tied directly to your project's construction timeline. Plan for renewal/extension if your timeline shifts.
