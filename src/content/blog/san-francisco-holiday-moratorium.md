Across the country, municipalities limit construction activity, building permit issuances, or street closures during certain dates and times through November and December. This is done to promote full access to retail stores, restaurants and other businesses by the public. 

The Holiday Moratorium in the City of San Francisco restricts work in the streets and sidewalks from the day after Thanksgiving **Friday, Nov. 28, 2025 through Thursday, Jan. 1, 2026** between the hours of 7am and 10pm. No work is allowed in the streets or sidewalks

1) in the area outlined in the “Holiday Restrictions Boundary” map in the Regulations for Working in San Francisco Streets (“Blue Book”); or

2) on any business block.

A “business block” is defined as a block in which at least 50% of the linear frontage is devoted to business and applies to blocks outside the map area. (Alleys are not included in the Holiday Moratorium restriction unless they meet the criteria of a “business block.” Alleys are defined as streets under 25’ wide from curb to curb).

Establishments in this protected category are retail stores, bars, restaurants, service type businesses, non-residence type hotels, or others as determined by the Director of Streets Division, for which the building includes a public entry on the subject street. Learn more [here.](https://www.sfmta.com/services/business-services/construction-regulations#:~:text=The%20Holiday%20Moratorium%20restricts%20work,hours%20of%207AM%20and%2010PM)

When does San Francisco's next Holiday Moratorium take effect? The same rule applies every year: the day after Thanksgiving through January 1. For the 2026 holiday season, that means the moratorium runs Friday, November 27, 2026 through Thursday, January 1, 2027, restricting work in the streets and sidewalks between 7am and 10pm. Does the Holiday Moratorium apply to every street in San Francisco? No - it applies specifically to streets within the "Holiday Restrictions Boundary" map in the SFMTA's Blue Book, plus any "business block" outside that map, defined as a block where at least 50% of the linear frontage is business-occupied (retail, restaurants, bars, service businesses, non-residence hotels, etc.). Are alleys exempt from the Holiday Moratorium? Generally yes, unless the alley itself qualifies as a business block. Alleys are defined as streets under 25 feet wide from curb to curb, and they're only swept into the restriction if they meet that 50%-business-frontage threshold. Can I still get a waiver to work during the moratorium period if my project is time-sensitive? Yes — exceptions are handled on a case-by-case basis through the SFMTA's Streets Division, and waiver requests typically need to be submitted with justification well ahead of the holiday window rather than after work has already started. Why do cities like San Francisco restrict construction during the holidays in the first place? The goal is to protect pedestrian and customer access to retail corridors during the year's highest-traffic shopping season - minimizing sidewalk and street obstructions from construction work when local businesses depend most on foot traffic.
