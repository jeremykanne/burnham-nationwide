## What is Due Diligence in Permitting?

Due diligence is the process of gathering, verifying, and analyzing the information required to submit permits successfully. It involves:

-   Researching jurisdictional requirements.
-   Confirming the specific documentation, drawings, and formats needed.
-   Communicating with city or county officials to ensure the most up-to-date compliance standards.

This groundwork is essential to avoid delays, ensure accurate submissions, and ultimately, move projects forward efficiently.  

## Why Early Due Diligence Matters

The earlier due diligence begins, the better. Permitting requirements often evolve, and missteps can result in costly delays. Missing even small details (that can widely vary depending on jurisdiction) can lead to submission rejections, impacting project timelines.

Starting due diligence early allows Burnham’s team to work closely with architects and engineers to ensure all materials meet the required standards before finalizing submissions.

## Common Challenges and Red Flags

One of the biggest hurdles in due diligence is navigating inconsistencies in jurisdictional requirements. For example, in one region, drawings might need to be submitted as individual PDF files with specific stamps, while another area accepts bundled files without additional verifications.

I once had a property changing its use type unexpectedly required site plan approval. Despite earlier assurances that it wasn’t necessary, a change in the review process delayed the project. Such instances highlight the importance of cross-verifying information from multiple sources and maintaining proactive communication with local agencies.

## Addressing Client Misconceptions

One common misconception is that due diligence services might be repetitive or unnecessary, particularly if clients have worked in a given area before. However, Burnham’s team emphasizes that permitting requirements frequently change, and assumptions based on past projects can lead to errors.

Clients should feel confident that every due diligence service Burnham provides is tailored to the specific project, ensuring compliance with current regulations. We’re not just cutting and pasting from prior projects.

What are common red flags that come up during permitting due diligence? Inconsistent jurisdictional requirements are the biggest one, for example, one city requiring individually stamped PDF drawings while a neighboring one accepts bundled files. How can cross-verifying information with multiple sources help avoid permitting delays? Jurisdictional review processes and requirements can shift even after an initial assurance is given, so confirming requirements through more than one source or contact at the agency helps catch changes before they derail a submission.
