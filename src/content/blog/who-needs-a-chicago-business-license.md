Large or small, if you're operating a business in Chicago, you'll most often need to acquire a City of Chicago business license. The City offers numerous resources for those applying for business licensing through their S[mall Business Center](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/bacp/sbc/small_business_centerhome.html) (SBC) in the Department of Business Affairs and Consumer Protection (BACP), where individuals may apply for new licenses, renew an existing license, or meet in-person with a Business Consultant during the application process. ![white-open-sign-with-book](./images/leyre-labarga-700581-unsplash-d998b64f.jpg)

<em>image by [Leyre Labarga](https://unsplash.com/photos/lfyP9zTgUp0?utm_source=unsplash&utm_medium=referral&utm_content=creditCopyText)</em>

The [SBC](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/bacp/sbc/business_licensing.html) "issues business licenses and regulates the business activities of industries as diverse as retail, food and liquor establishments, entertainment venues and theaters, day care centers, manufacturing facilities and motor vehicle repair shops", and is the central hub for most licensing in Chicago.

There are multiple tiers of business licenses, including Limited Business Licenses (LBL) that apply to all Chicago businesses not specifically exempt, Retail Food Licenses (RFL), Manufacturing Establishment Licenses (1009), and others as determined by the [BACP](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/narr/foia/key_performance_indicators0/business_benchmarks.html#rfl).

The majority of businesses must have a Chicago business license to operate within the city, however for some professions, if one is licensed by the [State of Illinois Department of Financial & Professional Regulation (IDFPR)](https://www.idfpr.com/), that person or associated business entity is not required to acquire a separate business license through the City of Chicago.

Although there are exceptions, the following are some of the professions that are issued State of Illinois licenses by the IDFPR and do not require Chicago licensing:

-   Architects
-   Engineers
-   Landscape Architects
-   Land Surveyors
-   Home Inspectors
-   Interior Designers
-   Real Estate Appraisers
-   Real Estate Brokerage  
    

Notable exception: If a company is operating a hair salon, barbershop, or nail salon in Chicago and is licensed through the IDFPR, that company must acquire a City of Chicago business license. A full list of licensed professions is available via the [IDFPR](http://www.idfpr.com/DPRdefault.asp).

## What are the steps to obtain a business license?

Prior to applying with the City of Chicago, [applicants](https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/bacp/general/3simplestepstoobtainbuslicf05313.pdf) must determine their legal entity (either a Sole Proprietor or General Partnership) and file for a DBA/Assumed Name Certificate with the Cook County Clerk's Office. If one will be doing business as a legal business entity - regardless of whether or not the company is Illinois-based - it is also required to file with the Illinois Secretary of State. 

Businesses must also obtain an Employer Identification Number (EIN) and an Illinois Department of Revenue (IDOR) Account ID Number prior to applying for a City of Chicago business license of any type.

## What's the cost of a limited business license?

In 2018, the Mayor and Chicago City Council passed an [ordinance](https://chicago.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=3510305&GUID=2E5ADA59-F6A7-48A5-A602-EBFD1D1DA162&Options=Advanced&Search=) that decreased the Limited Business License fee from $250.00 to $125.00 for new licenses. The change in fee structure is expected to sunset on June 30, 2019, and all new qualifying licenses will be issued at the reduced rate.

Other licenses have additional fees, up to $6,600 for a caterer's license with establishment located outside of the City of Chicago.

Most business licenses are valid for two years, and may require zoning review, site inspections, and a criminal background check. While only certain licenses can be applied for online, all renewals can be completed using the City's [portal](https://webapps1.cityofchicago.org/AccountServices/login.jsp).

## What are the penalties for running an unlicensed business in Chicago?

Penalties for not maintaining a Chicago business license can include a fine or a closure order issued to the business owner. Further, per Chapter 4 of the [Municipal Code](http://www.amlegal.com/codes/client/chicago_il/), unlicensed businesses are the liability of the property owner and fines will escalate with each subsequent violation. Both the business owner and the property owner are liable for unlicensed business practices, making it imperative that property owners ensure their tenants are licensed with the City.

## How many business licenses are in the City of Chicago?

There are over 60,000 active business licenses in the City of Chicago, and 21,000 of those licensees maintain a Limited Business License. These include:

-   250 licensed Mobile Food Trucks
-   635 licensed Commercial Parking Garages
-   758 licensed Public Place of Amusement venues
-   3,560 liquor licenses that allow consumption on premises
-   11,500 licensed retail food establishments  
    

The recent fee [adjustments](https://chicago.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=3510305&GUID=2E5ADA59-F6A7-48A5-A602-EBFD1D1DA162&Options=Advanced&Search=) are designed to make limited business licenses more accessible, allowing more individuals and entities to operated businesses within the city. 

## Pop-Up Initiative Creates New Licenses

The City of Chicago [Pop Up Initiative](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/bacp/supp_info/chicagopopupinitiative.html) - the first of its kind - went into effect December, 2018, creating a new category of business licenses for pop-up users and pop-up hosts. These licenses grant holders the ability to either "pop up" their business in various locations throughout Chicago for the duration of the license, or for hosts to allow pop-up businesses as tenants in their spaces. How much does a Chicago Limited Business License cost today? As of January 1, 2026, the Limited Business License fee is $500 for a two-year license - a fourfold increase from the $125 reduced fee this post cited, and double the original $250 fee before the 2018 reduction. The Regulated Business License also increased substantially to $1,000 for a two-year license. How many active business licenses are there in Chicago today? The city's open data portal, last updated June 21, 2026, shows approximately 53,300 active business licenses - meaningfully fewer than the "over 60,000" cited in this post, though the categories and counting methodology may differ. Do State of Illinois licensed professionals like architects and engineers still not need a separate Chicago business license? Yes - that exemption remains in place. Architects, engineers, landscape architects, land surveyors, and most other IDFPR-licensed professions don't need a separate city license, though the hair salon/barbershop/nail salon exception noted in this post also still applies. What happens if a business operates without a valid Chicago license? Both the business owner and the property owner can be held liable, with fines potentially running $500 to $1,000 per day for unlicensed operation, and any outstanding city debt must be resolved before a new license or renewal can be issued. Can all Chicago business license types be applied for online? Not all — while the Chicago Business Direct portal handles many license applications and all renewals, some license types still require an in-person visit to the Small Business Center at City Hall (121 N. LaSalle, Room 800), particularly for more complex license categories requiring consultations or inspections.
